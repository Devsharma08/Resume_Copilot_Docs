Evaluating Selection Paradigms: Dynamic Role-Model Benchmarking vs. Governed Closeness Metrics

Exploring how online dynamic selection (Priority Queues with continuous "Role Model" benchmark updates) interacts with hard policy enforcement, and how to measure true candidate "closeness" without falling victim to keyword gaming and semantic wordplay.

How Does Dynamic Role-Model Selection Support Our Research Goal?

Analyzing the user's proposed selection paradigm: Threshold Parameterization vs. Priority Queue with Dynamic "Role Model" Benchmark Replacement.

In operations research and computer science, your proposed selection mechanism connects directly to the Secretary Problem (Optimal Stopping Theory) and Online Priority Queues under Streaming Constraints. In traditional systems, decision-makers either set a fixed threshold (e.g., "accept anyone scoring > 80%") or maintain a top-K priority queue where the benchmark ("Role Model") is dynamically replaced whenever a superior candidate arrives.

Method A: Static Thresholding

Parametric
  • Mechanism: Set a pre-defined cutoff score $S_{min}$. Accept candidates who meet $S_{min}$ until capacity $K$ is filled.
  • Advantage: Fast, deterministic decision on arrival; order-independent.
  • Core Flaw in AI: Vulnerable to score drift. If the score is computed by a statistical LLM, a "keyword gamer" scores 95% early and occupies a slot, while better candidates arriving later might be rejected due to capacity limits.

Method B: Dynamic "Role Model" Priority Queue

Benchmark Replacement
  • Mechanism: Maintain a top-K Priority Queue. The candidate at $K_{max}$ becomes the current "Role Model". Any new applicant exceeding the Role Model displaces the lowest candidate in the queue.
  • Advantage: Continuous improvement; adaptively elevates the bar as pool quality increases.
  • New Risk Introduced: The False Role-Model Trap. If an adversarial candidate games the model with white-text or prompt injection, they become the "Role Model" benchmark, corrupting future evaluations.
⚠️ The Core Failure Condition in Un-Governed Selection

Neither static thresholding nor dynamic priority queues work if the Closeness / Perfection Metric is based on raw statistical language models (semantic embeddings or LLM match ratings). A keyword-stuffed resume will trick both methods. Therefore, the selection criteria must be strengthened before queuing can occur.

How to Measure "Closeness/Perfection" to a Job Role

Formulating a robust, governed distance metric that replaces raw vector similarity with multi-tier policy admissibility.

The Flaw of Standard Cosine Similarity

Standard ATS and LLM screeners rely on vector cosine similarity: Sim(Resume, Job) = (v_r · v_j) / (||v_r|| ||v_j||). This measures keyword overlap and semantic proximity, but completely fails to detect false claims, missing certifications, or prompt injections.

The Governed Admissibility Distance Metric ($M_G$)

// Multi-Tier Governed Closeness Score Formula:
M_G(C, J) = P_hard(C, J) × [ w1·E_provenance(C) + w2·S_semantic(C, J) - w3·U_uncertainty(C) ]
Where:
• P_hard(C,J) ∈ {0, 1}: Deterministic Boolean Gate (Hard Requirements). If fail, score = 0.
• E_provenance(C) ∈ [0, 1]: Verified Evidence Ratio (Claims backed by proof).
• S_semantic(C,J) ∈ [0, 1]: Contextual semantic match on verified text only.
• U_uncertainty(C) ∈ [0, 1]: Epistemic uncertainty penalty.

Integrating Dynamic Selection into the Governed AI Vision

How to capture these thoughts for future expansion in your PhD / research architecture.

Pillar 1: Deterministic Policy Gating

Out-of-Model Guard

Before any applicant enters the Priority Queue or is evaluated against the "Role Model", they must pass an out-of-model Open Policy Agent (OPA) boolean check ($P_{hard}$). This eliminates invalid candidates before statistical scoring begins.

Pillar 2: Evidence Provenance Ledger

Verified Claim Scoring

Instead of measuring raw prompt text, extract atomic claims (e.g., "5 years Python", "AWS Certified") and verify source evidence. Closeness is computed only on claims with high provenance confidence ($E_{provenance}$).

Pillar 3: Dynamic Policy-Bounded Queue

Order-Independent Streaming

By combining $M_G$ scoring with dynamic Priority Queues, candidate intake becomes resilient to streaming order, preventing early keyword gamers from displacing late-arriving verified experts.

Formalized Research Contribution Framing

"We present the Policy-Bounded Priority Queue (PBPQ) combined with a Multi-Tier Governed Admissibility Distance ($M_G$). This architecture proves that online candidate selection can remain adaptive and benchmark-driven while guaranteeing zero violations of organizational hard constraints and complete immunity to semantic keyword gaming."
